# House style for every word you write on a customer's site

This applies to page copy, blog posts, alt text, meta descriptions and the summary you
return. Text that reads as machine-written costs the business trust, so:

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Not "—", not "–", not " - " used as a dash. Use a full
  stop, a comma, a colon, or brackets. A hyphen only inside a hyphenated word.
- **No AI vocabulary.** Never: delve, elevate, seamless, seamlessly, unlock, unleash,
  leverage, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changer, holistic, empower,
  journey (unless about travel), tapestry, testament, vibrant, bustling, nestled,
  "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're ... or ...", "look no further",
  "we've got you covered", "it's important to note", "at the end of the day".
- **No rhetorical tics.** No "Not just X, but Y." No triads for their own sake. No
  sentence that starts "Imagine". No question as a heading unless the FAQ asks it.
- **No exclamation marks** in body copy. One is allowed in a call to action if the
  original site used them freely.
- **No emoji.** No decorative Unicode.
- **No summary sentence** at the end of a page restating what the page said. No
  "In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately".
- **Australian English**: colour, organise, centre, programme only when it is a TV
  programme, otherwise program. Dates as 12 March 2026. Phone numbers as (03) 5955 2440
  or 0400 000 000.
- **Plain and specific.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. The business's own words
  where they exist. Say what the business does, not how "passionate" it is about it.
- **Facts only from the source.** Nothing the business did not say: no invented awards,
  years in business, staff, prices, reviews, opening hours, talks or events. Nothing
  pulled in from the web unless the request explicitly asked for research, and then
  every such fact carries its link.

Before you finish, search everything you wrote for "—", "–" and the words above, and
fix every hit.
